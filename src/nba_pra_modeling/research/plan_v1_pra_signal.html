Points + Rebounds + Assists (PRA) — Signal Investigation Plan v1

2026-05-25  |  Market: player_points_rebounds_assists  |  First pass, all lines

Context & Motivation

PRA is untested. It's structurally different from individual stat markets: it aggregates three separate count stats, each with its own variance. This creates more surface area for mispricing.

AttributeIndividual stats (PTS/REB/AST/STL)PRA
Line rangeNarrow (e.g. steals 0.5–1.5)Wide: 15.5–45.5 (2024-25 distribution roughly uniform 18.5–35.5)
VarianceLower — single statHigher — sum of 3 stats, each noisy
Market pricingMarket is efficient on pure-counting statsMay be less efficient when role changes affect the mix (e.g. PTS↓ but AST↑)
Regression to meanStrong on individual statsStronger on PRA — a 40-PRA outlier game almost always regresses hard
The PRA market may lag when a player's role shifts (e.g. more ball-handling → more AST, fewer PTS from iso plays). The total PRA may be similar but the mix changes and the line was set for the old mix.

Hypotheses to Test

  1. Regression after outlier games: After a player hits 40+ PRA in a game, the next line is anchored too high. Bet the under. Same as the "high-line regression" hypothesis in assists — but PRA variance is even larger so the effect should be stronger.
  2. Calibration gap by line tier: The uniform line distribution (every 1-point increment gets ~3.5-4% of rows) suggests books set lines by formula. Check if any tier (under-20, 20-30, 30+) is systematically mispriced. Under-20 is likely role players who shouldn't have high PRA lines.
  3. Minutes sensitivity: Like steals and rebounds, PRA is a volume stat. A player averaging 30 PRA in 36 minutes who plays 24 minutes will fall well short of their line. The same oracle/pre-game test from steals applies.
  4. Post-injury return: Players returning from injury tend to have lower PRA in their first 2-3 games but the market sets the line near their season average. Classic under opportunity.
  5. Role mix change: When a player's PTS/AST mix shifts (e.g. becomes primary ball-handler), their PRA total may stay the same but individual stat tracking lags. The market prices PTS and AST individually and sums — if it lags on one component, the PRA line will be wrong.

Cell 9 — Regression after outlier games hypothesis

# Does betting under after a player had a big PRA game show edge?
dc = df_clean.sort_values(["PLAYER_NAME","season","game_date"]).copy()
dc["pra_last1"] = dc.groupby(["PLAYER_NAME","season"])["PRA"].shift(1)
dc["pra_roll10"] = dc.groupby(["PLAYER_NAME","season"])["PRA"].transform(
    lambda s: s.shift(1).rolling(10, min_periods=5).mean()
)

# "Outlier game" = last game PRA was 1.3x+ the rolling average
dc["last_game_vs_avg"] = dc["pra_last1"] / dc["pra_roll10"].replace(0, float("nan"))
dc["outlier_bucket"] = pd.cut(dc["last_game_vs_avg"],
    bins=[0, 0.7, 0.9, 1.1, 1.3, 10],
    labels=["cold (<0.7x)", "below avg", "on avg", "above avg", "hot (>1.3x)"]
)

print("=== Under ROI by last-game PRA vs rolling average ===")
print(dc.dropna(subset=["outlier_bucket"]).groupby("outlier_bucket", observed=True)[["y_under","pnl"]].agg(
    count=("pnl","count"), hit_rate=("y_under","mean"), roi=("pnl","mean")
).round(3))
Key test: if a player had a hot game (1.3x their average), does the under hit more on the next game? This is the regression hypothesis.

Cell 10 — Pre-game proxy (same test as steals)

# Can we predict with pre-game info who will go under?
dc["pra_roll10_vs_line"] = dc["pra_roll10"] - dc["prop_line"]
# Positive = player's avg > line (market set it low), negative = avg < line (market set it high)

print("=== Under ROI by (rolling PRA - prop_line) ===")
dc["avg_vs_line"] = pd.cut(dc["pra_roll10_vs_line"],
    bins=[-50, -5, -2, 0, 2, 5, 50],
    labels=["avg << line", "avg < line", "avg ≈ line-", "avg ≈ line+", "avg > line", "avg >> line"]
)
print(dc.dropna(subset=["avg_vs_line"]).groupby("avg_vs_line", observed=True)[["y_under","pnl"]].agg(
    count=("pnl","count"), hit_rate=("y_under","mean"), roi=("pnl","mean")
).round(3))
