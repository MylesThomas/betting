NBA Points — Dispersion Research Plan

📅 2026-05-26 👤 Thomas Myles 📂 scripts/20260526_dispersion.py 🔗 Follows v1 Points Post-Mortem

Motivation

v1 points modeling found the book line alone captures 97.97% of GBM feature importance. Rolling-average features don't bring information the market doesn't already have, and walk-forward ROI couldn't clear the 6.9% vig.

Core Shift
Rather than predicting a player's points in isolation (a nearly-efficient market), the exploitable signal may live in the relationship between the aggregate (team total) and the distribution (individual props) — i.e., the dispersion framework from volatility trading.

The Finance Parallel

In equity markets, dispersion trading exploits the gap between implied correlation and realized correlation across index constituents:

σ²index = Σᵢ Σⱼ wᵢ · wⱼ · ρᵢⱼ · σᵢ · σⱼ Dispersion trade: Long single-stock vol + Short index vol Profits when: Realized correlation < Implied correlation

The structural premium persists because institutional demand for index puts inflates implied correlation. Single-stock options carry no equivalent demand pressure.

Key Difference
Unlike stocks, NBA players are subject to a hard budget constraint: possessions are zero-sum. When a star over-performs their line, teammates must collectively under-perform. This creates a structural negative correlation that finance dispersion doesn't have.

Research Questions

RQ1 — Dispersion Structure

  • What is the empirical distribution of within-team scoring concentration (CV, HHI, top-1 share, Gini) across 3 seasons?
  • Do teams have stable, persistent dispersion profiles, or is it game-by-game noise?
  • Is concentration driven by roster composition (one superstar) or game state (who-got-hot)?

RQ2 — Intra-Team Player Correlation

  • Are co-starters on the same team negatively correlated in points (zero-sum budget), positively correlated (pace/foul-rate effects), or near zero?
  • Does correlation sign/magnitude differ by role pair (star+star vs. star+role player)?

RQ3 — Dispersion Predictability

  • Does a team's rolling realized dispersion predict next-game dispersion? (lag-1 HHI autocorrelation)
  • Does a star's recent over-performance predict near-term teammate under-performance?

RQ4 — Signal for Individual Player Lines

  • Can dispersion metrics (team concentration, player share, correlation regime) add predictive power to individual player point outcomes?
  • Do UNDER bets on co-starters after a star's dominant performance show positive expected value?

Dispersion Metrics Defined

MetricFormulaInterpretationRange
CV (coefficient of variation) std(pts) / mean(pts) Relative spread of scoring. High = uneven distribution. 0 – ∞ (typically 0.4–1.2)
HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman) Σ (pts_i / team_pts)² Concentration index. 1.0 = one player scores all; 1/n = perfectly equal. 1/n – 1.0 (typically 0.08–0.30)
Top-1 Share max(pts) / team_pts Fraction of team scoring by top scorer. 0 – 1.0 (typically 0.20–0.45)
Top-3 Share sum(3 highest pts) / team_pts Combined share of the three leading scorers. 0 – 1.0 (typically 0.50–0.75)
Gini Coefficient Area between Lorenz curve and equality line Inequality of scoring distribution. 0 = equal; 1 = maximum concentration. 0 – 1.0 (typically 0.35–0.65)

Hypotheses

  • H1 Within a team-game, the top-1 scorer captures >30% of team points on average.
  • H2 Teams with a superstar (player avg >25 ppg) show HHI >0.18 vs ~0.13 for balanced teams.
  • H3 Median pairwise intra-team correlation is negative (−0.05 to −0.15). The zero-sum possession budget creates structural negative co-movement.
  • H4 [Core signal] On nights a star posts a top-decile game, their co-starters average a negative residual vs their own rolling mean (<−1.5 pts).
  • H5 Rolling HHI is weakly predictive of individual player point outcomes, independent of the book line. (Feature correlation test in Section 6.)

At n=1,000, a two-tailed z-test can distinguish 53.5% from 50% with ~80% power. If H4 shows teammates average −2 pts on star nights (vs ~0 baseline), and props are priced to the rolling mean, this points to a structural edge on the under.

Key Caveat Throughout
Sections 6 and 7 use actual prop lines from the src/nba_data cache where available (median across bookmakers). Lines are missing for ~48% of player-games; those rows fall back to the rolling-10 mean proxy. Two remaining open risks before fully trusting S7 ROI figures: (1) selection bias — props only exist for established rotation players, so the bet universe is non-random; (2) name normalization — mismatches between normalize_from_nba_api and normalize_from_odds_api silently drop rows or corrupt is_under. Line timing is confirmed closing; since the book may partially adjust teammate lines after a star's dominant prior game, our measured edge is likely a lower bound on the true opening-line edge.

Section 1 — Data Overview
73,893 player-game rows across 3 seasons (23,984 / 23,995 / 25,914). Mean points remarkably stable: 11.63 → 11.59 → 11.52 pts. Median is 10 pts in all seasons — heavy right skew driven by stars. Slight trend toward more balanced scoring (declining CV and HHI year-over-year).
Section 2 — Team-Game Dispersion Metrics
Mean HHI = 0.157 (vs perfectly equal = 0.083 for 12-player roster → teams are roughly 2× more concentrated than equal). Top-1 player captures 24.5% of scoring, top-3 capture 57.6%. All metrics are declining slightly across seasons — the league is getting marginally more balanced. HHI and team_pts show weak negative correlation (r ≈ −0.04): high-concentration games very slightly lower-scoring.
Section 3 — Concentration Stability
Lag-1 HHI autocorrelation median = 0.040 — barely any game-to-game persistence. Concentration is mostly noise, not a persistent property of a given night. However, team identity is highly persistent: Lakers, Knicks, Bucks rank concentrated all 3 seasons; Pacers, Wizards, Nets rank balanced all 3 seasons. The team-level profile is stable; the game-level realization is noisy.

Section 5 — Star Over-Performance → Teammate Response ⭐
H4 confirmed, but effect is smaller than hoped.

Using threshold of +10.1 pts above rolling-10 mean (1.5σ), identified 1,482 "star nights." Teammates on those nights averaged −0.20 pts vs their rolling mean, compared to +0.02 pts baseline. Effect = −0.22 pts (t=−3.41, p=0.0006). Top-decile nights (resid > 8.6 pts, n=2,031): effect = −0.21 pts, p=0.0001.

The signal is real and significant with n=12,379 observations. However, −0.22 pts is well below the −1.5 pt threshold set as the minimum actionable effect size. This is unlikely to overcome the 6.9% vig on its own. Needs to be layered with other signals (role player context, opponent defense, minutes projection) before it's tradeable.

⚠️ Section 7 Caveats — Requires Scrutiny Before Trusting
  • Selection bias risk: Props only exist for 52% of player-games (bets drop from 11,627 → 4,993 vs rolling mean). We're only betting on players with posted props — likely established rotation players. If some were injury-DNPs the book removed, the remaining props may be biased toward under outcomes.
  • Line timing — closing lines confirmed: The stored prop_line reflects the closing line. If the book moves teammate lines down in response to a star's prior dominant game, we're testing against an already-adjusted line — this would understate the true edge (the book is partially correcting), but the residual edge we see is real.
  • Name normalization: Logs use normalize_from_nba_api, props use normalize_from_odds_api. Any mismatches silently drop rows (inflating prop_line NaN rate) or mis-match players (corrupt is_under).
