From yhat to edge — pricing chain

yhat_adj = consensus_line + (1 − shrink) × (yhat − consensus_line) ← shrink ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5}
σ = roll_reb_std_5 (per-player) or global residual std fallback
P(under) = norm.cdf(line, loc=yhat_adj, scale=σ)
edge = P(under) − implied_prob(under_odds) ← bet when edge > min_edge
Consensus line acts as the shrinkage anchor — the market's prior on yhat. Leaves tagged gap are drivers of the true outcome that the current model doesn't see; each is a potential feature to add.

Forward-looking dynamics

Trends that affect which drivers matter most, and how much. Not part of the static structure.

Signal strength

Role change lag in 60g window
Long rolling window dampens response to trades, minutes restrictions, and coaching changes — blending a short (10-15g) window is the highest-value near-term improvement
Data source: rebounds_feature_spec.py / S3 feature universe
Position signal untapped
C/PF rebound at ~3-4× the per-minute rate of guards; position is a strong prior that the current model infers only indirectly via the rolling mean
Data source: NBA API roster data (TBD)
σ underestimates returning players
5-game std is thin after an injury return; manual override or a floor on σ prevents overconfident edges in those cases
Data source: edge_calibration.py model residuals

Game volume drivers

3PA rate structural rise
League 3PA share has grown from ~30% to ~40% of FGA over the past decade; 3s miss at ~66% and rebound further, inflating total boards and making roll_fg3a a richer signal over time
Data source: NBA API shot chart data (TBD)
Game total as pace proxy
O/U total is correlated with expected pace and total shot volume; adding it alongside 3PA rate may reduce reliance on lagged rolling stats for volume estimation
Data source: The Odds API

Competition / matchup

Opponent REB rate unstable
Team defensive rebounding rates fluctuate 5-8% across stretches within a season due to rotation and injury; point-in-time rates outperform season averages as matchup inputs
Data source: NBA API team stats (TBD)
Lineup data sparsity
On-court teammate rebounding overlap requires lineup-level data which is available from NBA API but expensive to join at scale; high value, moderate engineering cost
Data source: NBA API lineup endpoint (TBD)
Reading the tree: yhat = Expected Minutes × Per-Min REB Rate. Minutes scales the entire prediction — a player who plays 20 minutes simply has fewer chances than one who plays 35. Per-min rate decomposes into what the player tends to do (tendency), how many boards are available (game volume), and how contested those boards are (competition).
