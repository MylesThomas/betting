# Example: From Research to Post

## How to Use Your Research to Write Better Narratives

### Step 1: Do Your Research

**Game:** Patriots @ Ravens (Week 16)

**Search Queries Used:**
1. "Patriots Week 16 2025 injury report"
2. "Ravens Week 16 2025 injury report"
3. "Patriots recent performance last 3 games"
4. "Ravens 24-0 shutout Week 15 opponent"
5. "Patriots Ravens betting line movement Week 16"

**Research Notes:**
```
Ravens:
- Beat [OPPONENT] 24-0 in Week 15
- [OPPONENT]'s QB [NAME] left game early with injury
- Lamar Jackson: [STATUS]
- Defense: [KEY INJURIES IF ANY]

Patriots:
- Lost to Bills 31-[SCORE] in Week 15
- Drake Maye: [STATUS]
- Offensive line: [INJURIES]
- Recent trend: [COMPETITIVE/BLOWOUTS?]

Betting:
- Opening line: Ravens -[X]
- Current line: Ravens -3.0
- Movement: [DIRECTION AND WHY]
```

### Step 2: Your Luck Data

From your model:
- NE unlucky: -5.7
- BAL lucky: +18.6
- Luck differential: 24.3 (huge!)
- Bet: NE +3.0

### Step 3: Write the Narrative (BEFORE - What You Had)

❌ **Original (Fabricated):**
> Baltimore's 24-0 shutout was inflated—boosted by facing a backup QB after an early injury. New England was unlucky despite hanging 31 points on Buffalo in a competitive shootout. At +3, we're fading an overvalued shutout and backing a Patriots team that's better than last week's result suggests.

**Problems:**
- Vague details ("a backup QB" - who?)
- Assumed it was "competitive" without checking
- No specific context about either team

### Step 4: Write the Narrative (AFTER - With Research)

✅ **Improved (Research-Based):**
> Baltimore's 24-0 shutout of [ACTUAL OPPONENT] looks dominant, but context matters: [OPPONENT QB NAME] left the game in the first quarter with [INJURY], and the Ravens faced a third-string QB for three quarters. Meanwhile, New England [ACTUAL CONTEXT FROM RESEARCH - e.g., "kept it close with Buffalo until the fourth quarter" or "lost by 10 but covered the spread"]. The efficiency numbers tell a different story than the scoreboard—Baltimore's +18.6 luck rating is the highest of the season, while New England's -5.7 suggests they played better than the result. At +3, we're buying low on the Patriots and fading a Ravens performance that won't repeat.

**Why it's better:**
- Specific opponent and QB name
- Actual game context (when QB left, who replaced him)
- Real details about Patriots performance
- Still uses your luck data as the contrarian angle
- Sounds informed, not fabricated

### Step 5: The Formula

For each play, follow this structure:

1. **Context the favorite's performance** (with real details)
   - Who did they play?
   - What happened in the game?
   - Why might it be misleading?

2. **Context the underdog's performance** (with real details)
   - What actually happened?
   - Were there mitigating factors?
   - What does the box score miss?

3. **Introduce your edge** (the luck data)
   - "The efficiency numbers tell a different story..."
   - "Our model sees regression coming..."
   - "The market is overreacting to..."

4. **Make the case** (why this line is wrong)
   - Combine narrative + data
   - Explain the value
   - State the bet

## Bottom Line

**Don't write:**
> "Team X was unlucky in a loss that looked worse than it was."

**Do write:**
> "Team X lost 24-17 to [OPPONENT] but outgained them 380-290 and lost on two red zone turnovers. QB [NAME] completed 68% of his passes despite [CONTEXT]. The efficiency metrics show..."

**The difference:** Specific, verifiable details that show you actually researched the game.
