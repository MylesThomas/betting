**Tweet 2:**
```
it's hardly the book's job to price these markets correctly — it's just a game of seeing how much bettors will tolerate.

example: the rams at +336 avg. (+360 on fanduel) should be +417. that's a 3.6% vig, or a 19% upcharge on the fair price.

this is what happens with "yes-only" markets: no two-way pricing, so the vig gets baked in and your average joe doesn't notice.
```

**Tweet 3:**
```
and it's not just one book - every book does this.

the "best" odds for contenders still have 1-2% vig baked in, per team.

and if a team is public or a heavy favorite? you pay even more.
```

**Tweet 4:**
```
the rams, seahawks, broncos, bills, and eagles all have 1.5-3%+ edges to the house, per team.

the only exceptions? unpopular teams like the jaguars (-0.2%) and chargers (-0.1%) actually have negative vig at fanduel - a rare find in futures markets.

but on average across all books, only extreme longshots like the cowboys (avg. 600-1 odds) have fair pricing because nobody's betting them anyway.
```
