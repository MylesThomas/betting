Title: The Hidden Tax on NFL Championship Futures

Subtitle: Bookmakers Charge 18.5% Vig on Super Bowl Bets—Here's Why It Matters

If you've ever bet on NFL futures, you know the appeal: lock in a team early, root for them all season, and potentially cash big in February. But there's a hidden cost most bettors never calculate: futures markets carry massive vig compared to game lines.

I analyzed championship odds across all major sportsbooks for every NFL team. The findings are stark.

---
The Vig Gap

- Average vig on futures: 18.5%
- Typical game line vig: 4-5%

That's a 4x markup. You're paying 18.5% just to place the bet—before accounting for any skill edge you might have.

For context, a standard -110/-110 game line has roughly 4.8% vig. Futures? Nearly four times higher.

---
Why Bookmakers Get Away With It

Futures are typically "yes-only" markets. You can't bet against a team winning the Super Bowl in the same way you bet against a point spread. Without two-way pricing to keep them honest, bookmakers bake in the vig and most bettors don't notice.

It's hardly the book's job to price these markets efficiently—it's just a game of seeing how much bettors will tolerate.

---
The Numbers: Where You're Paying Most

Take the Los Angeles Rams, currently averaging +336 across all books (+360 on FanDuel). When you remove the vig and normalize probabilities to 100%, the fair odds should be +417. 

That's a 3.6% vig—or a 19% upcharge on the fair price. For every $100 you bet, $3.60 goes straight to the house before the season even plays out.

And it's not just one book. Every major sportsbook does this:
- Rams, Seahawks, Broncos, Bills, Eagles: 1.5-3.6% vig per team
- Packers, Patriots, 49ers: 1.1-1.3% vig per team
- Mid-tier longshots: 0.5-1.0% vig per team

The "best" odds for contenders still have 1-2% vig baked in, per team. If a team is public or a heavy favorite, you pay even more.

---
The Rare Exceptions

There are two notable outliers: Jaguars (-0.2% vig) and Chargers (-0.1% vig) at FanDuel actually offer negative vig—meaning you're getting better-than-fair odds. This is exceptionally rare in futures markets.

These are unpopular teams with moderate playoff odds. FanDuel is likely using them to balance their book or attract action away from the favorites.

Beyond that, the only teams with near-fair pricing are extreme longshots like the Cowboys (averaging +72,614) where books know nobody's betting them anyway. When there's no action, there's no reason to charge vig.

---
What This Means for Your Betting Strategy

1. Line shopping helps, but it won't save you.
Even the best odds across all books have 1-2% vig for contenders. Shopping is worth it, but you're still paying a premium.

2. Futures are entertainment bets.
If you love rooting for a team all season, go for it. Just understand you're paying 18.5% on average for that privilege. It's like buying an extended lottery ticket.

3. Game-by-game betting has better math.
The 4-5% vig on individual game lines is expensive, but it's a bargain compared to futures. If your goal is +EV betting, stick to weekly markets.

4. Negative vig opportunities are gold.
If you believe in the Jaguars or Chargers, FanDuel is offering a rare fair price. These yellow-highlighted cells in the visualization above don't come around often.

---
The Methodology

Fair probabilities were calculated by taking the average implied probability across all seven major books (FanDuel, DraftKings, BetMGM, BetRivers, Bovada, BetOnline, LowVig) and normalizing to sum to exactly 100%.

This removes the overround (vig) and gives us the "true" market probability for each team. The difference between implied and fair probability is the vig you're paying.

---
The Bottom Line

Bookmakers love futures because they can charge higher margins without bettors noticing. The visualization above shows every team's fair odds versus what books charge—some teams carry 3-4% vig per bet.

Want to bet smarter? Focus on individual games, shop aggressively for the best lines, and treat futures as entertainment rather than your core strategy. And if you do bet futures, at least know what you're paying for the privilege.
