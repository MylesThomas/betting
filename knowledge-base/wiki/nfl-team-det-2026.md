## Schedule & rest

- **SoS**: **#1 easiest schedule in NFL** — dramatic advantage
- Dome team; plays almost entirely in controlled conditions after Week 4
- Schedule structure heavily favors the Lions in the second half of the season

---

## The 2025 context: injuries masked talent

Detroit went 9-8 in 2025 despite being the **#2 most-injured team in the NFL**:

- **#32 fumble luck** (-7.0 net fumbles vs expectation — historically bad)
- **#30 field goal luck** (-4.5 net vs expectation)
- **3-5 in one-score games** — bad luck in close situations

The team was undermined by systemic bad luck on top of an injury avalanche. The underlying talent and scheme were not reflected in the 9-8 record.

---

## Jared Goff's case

Goff is arguably the most underrated QB in the NFL by the market:

- **68% completion rate, 7.9 YPA, 34 TDs, 8 INTs** (2025)
- **Top-5 in all stable metrics**: EPA/att, success rate, YPA, completion percentage
- **3 straight seasons with 30+ TDs** — elite floor
- **#3 EPA/att from behind shotgun** (the Lions' primary formation)

His game operates within a system, but the system is built around maximizing his strengths. With Petzing inheriting rather than redesigning, continuity is expected.

---

## The key losses

**Ben Johnson to Chicago**: The creative mind behind one of the most efficient offenses in the NFL. Petzing inherits the scheme but has never been a head play-caller. The continuity question is real.

**Jahmyr Gibbs to Houston**: Gibbs was arguably the best RB in the NFL in 2025. **Isiah Pacheco ranked 46th/47th in every efficiency metric among 49 qualifying RBs** — this is the sharpest positional downgrade of any playoff contender this offseason. The #2-ranked RB unit ranking (carryover from Gibbs era) does not reflect this reality.

**OL turnover**: Ragnow (C) retired; Zeitler (G) departed. The rebuilt OL drops from elite to functional (#14); how quickly new starters gel matters.

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- SoS is **#1 easiest in NFL** — plays into the Lions' home-dome identity
- Goff is top-5 at the position; 30+ TDs is his floor
- 2025 record (9-8) badly understates true talent level: #2 most injured, #32 fumble luck, #30 FG luck, 3-5 in one-score games — all expect regression toward Lions
- Hutchinson is fully healthy; Front 7 is legitimately elite
- Receivers still #2 — St. Brown and Williams are untouched
- Fumble luck alone (regression from -7.0) worth 2-3 wins

**Under argument**
- Lost the most innovative OC in the NFL (Johnson → CHI) and have a first-time play-caller
- Lost the best RB on the roster (Gibbs → HOU) and replaced him with the 46th-ranked RB in efficiency
- Both starting safeties (Branch, Joseph) may miss significant time; secondary is health-dependent
- Kelvin Sheppard has never called defensive plays at any level
- 10.5 wins is a high bar — requires executing on a new OC's first season and a rebuilt backfield

**Sharp lean**: Neutral. The schedule advantage is the most significant factor in the NFL, and the 2025 bad luck argues strongly for positive regression. But losing Johnson and Gibbs simultaneously — the two best players at their craft on offense — is a real concern that isn't fully priced in.

## Prop angles

- **Jared Goff**: TDs over, YPA over — stable metrics career player; Petzing inherited the system; 34 TDs is the reasonable floor facing the easiest schedule in the NFL.
- **Amon-Ra St. Brown**: Target share over — becomes even more important as the offense adjusts to a new play-caller; slot role doesn't change.
- **Aidan Hutchinson**: Sacks over — 14.5 in a comeback year fully healthy; elite pass rusher on an elite Front 7; 15-17 sack ceiling is real.
- **Isiah Pacheco**: Fade all efficiency metrics (YPC over, long rush over) — he is a clear downgrade from Gibbs; volume may be there (attempts over is reasonable) but per-carry props are a trap.
- **Game totals**: Lions home game overs — easiest schedule, elite offense, dome environment. Neutral on totals against top-5 defenses where new OC is learning.
