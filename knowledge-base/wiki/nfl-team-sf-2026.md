## 2025: the most impressive coaching job of the Shanahan era

The 49ers started 2025 as the **#5 most-injured team** and still went 12-5. Among those injured for meaningful stretches: Purdy (QB, 8 games), Kittle (TE, 5+ games), Aiyuk (WR, PUP all season), Pearsall (WR, PCL), Bosa (EDGE, ACL Week 6), Williams (EDGE, ACL Week 9), Warner (LB, ankle).

**The qualification**: significant luck helped them survive:
- **FG luck: +8.6 (#2)** — own FG 91.7% (#7), but opponents made only **66.7%** of FGs against them (#1 best) — opponents missed 6.54 FGs over expected (most in NFL)
- **1-score games: 5-1 (.833, #2)** — extremely regressive
- **Penalty margin: -24 (#3 best)** — clean discipline throughout

---

## The Purdy concerns heading into 2026

Despite the overall efficiency numbers, there are warning signs:

**Play-action collapse**: The Shanahan staple that worked beautifully in 2024 (+0.29 EPA/att, #7) fell apart in 2025 (-0.10 EPA/att, #32). The difference was target depth:
- 2024 PA: 9.2 air yards, sack+INT rate 7.8%
- 2025 PA: 5.7 air yards, sack+INT rate **12%**

Shanahan dialed up play action to 40% of early-down dropbacks (5th in NFL) — higher usage with worse results, likely because the receiving corps lacked the personnel to push the ball deep off PA.

**Air yards regression**: From 8.6 (#11) in 2024 to 7.6 (#27) in 2025. The 49ers were heavily reliant on air yards rather than YAC (61% of yardage from air in 2025, #3 highest among QBs).

**Mike Evans and the YAC problem**: The hope is Evans + Stribling restore the physical, contested-catch profile Shanahan craves — which should improve YAC numbers.

---

## John Lynch: the draft problem

Lynch's draft capital efficiency ranking over 4 years: #31, #28, #31, **#32** (2026). In that span, he drafted 23 players after Round 1 — 15 were reaches. The 49ers can't absorb these misses when 3-4 starters get injured simultaneously.

Sharp's framing: "I can only imagine what Kyle Shanahan could do with a proper general manager drafting these players."

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- Shanahan is HC #3 — he coached this team to 12 wins while losing Purdy, Kittle, Bosa, Williams, and Warner for significant stretches
- **Health regression to mean**: were #28 in player health in 2025; law of averages says 2026 should be healthier; Bosa + Williams + Warner + Greenlaw all return
- Purdy's 3rd-down efficiency (#1 in NFL) and red zone efficiency (#1) are scheme-driven and repeatable
- Eddy Piñeiro's 97% FG rate is partly real; Lynch accidentally found a good kicker when he cut his own bad draft pick
- OL (#7) with Trent Williams at LT remains among the best in football
- #8 easiest schedule; #1 best for games off road SNF/MNF and negated bye

**Under argument**
- **FG luck +8.6 and 1-score record 5-1 (.833) are highly regressive** — losing just 2-3 of those close calls produces 9-10 wins
- Veteran age risk: Williams (38), Kittle (33), Evans (33), McCaffrey (30), Bosa (29) — multiple players returning from major injuries at advanced ages
- Secondary is **#26** — the only unit with a clear structural hole; opposing QBs will target it relentlessly
- Play action collapsing while Shanahan leaned into it harder (#5 in usage) signals a scheme-personnel mismatch that doesn't auto-correct
- CMC overuse (311 rushes, 75 RZ rushes) at age 30 coming off a "comeback" season is a significant injury risk
- Kittle and Warner returning from Achilles/ankle at ages 33+ adds more injury variance
- John Lynch's #32 draft capital efficiency means the roster is thin behind starters

**Sharp lean (Grade A+)**: "The law of averages says San Francisco is due for a relatively healthy run... If that scenario unfolds, they will be legitimate Super Bowl contenders and strong candidates to capture that elusive Lombardi Trophy." Slight Over. The roster talent level (five top-10 units) is legitimately elite when healthy. But this franchise has burned bettors with injury variance more than any other team in recent NFL history.

## Prop angles

- **Christian McCaffrey**: Rush yards over early-season — Shanahan promises to reduce his load, but CMC's target share in passing game will stay elite; fade full-season props until training camp injury update
- **Mike Evans**: Touchdown over, yards moderate — Evans' red zone dominance (58.3% of end zone targets) is real; but age/hamstring history makes full-season yardage props risky; targets will flow when Kittle and Pearsall miss time
- **George Kittle**: Game-by-game targets over — when healthy, TE success rate #3 in NFL; his injury history makes full-season props require aggressive discounting
- **Brock Purdy**: 3rd down props — his 3rd-down efficiency (#1 in NFL) is scheme-embedded and repeatable; when healthy and in scheme, he converts at an elite rate
- **Team totals**: Lean Over in early-season home games vs non-divisional opponents when health is intact; be cautious on totals in divisional games vs SEA and LAR which have strong defenses
