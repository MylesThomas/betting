Flagged Items

Flagged 1 — line_x_pos_RB is the real winner: consensus_point × pos_RB — the market line multiplied by a 1/0 RB flag. For an RB with a line of 18: 18 × 1 = 18. For a QB with a line of 18: 18 × 0 = 0. The key insight: the same line number means something categorically different by position. An RB at line=18 is a bell-cow getting carries by design — reliable, tightly distributed around the line. A QB at line=18 (e.g. a scrambler in a specific scheme) has the same number but a completely different variance and outcome distribution. Without the interaction, Ridge fits one coefficient for consensus_point across all positions. With line_x_pos_RB, it can learn a steeper slope specifically for RBs — "trust the market line more for RBs; apply more skepticism for non-RBs." This is why it jumps to the #2 individual predictor (RMSE 4.308 vs consensus_point at 4.028).

Flagged 2 — Bucket combos hurt vs simple continuous features: carry_buckets_market (RMSE 4.042) is worse than carry_L8_market (RMSE 4.028). The dummies are collinear with consensus_point and add noise. They work better as individual features than in combination with the market line.

Flagged 3 — carry_trend has zero signal: R²=−0.006 individually. Momentum effects in other prop markets (e.g., passing yards) don't appear to carry over to rush attempts — carry count is determined by role and matchup more than short-term form.

Step 4 — Outcome Distribution → Binary Bet Signal

Status: All 5 tests PASS

P(over) Method

Target is continuous (carries count), so P(over) is derived from the OOF residual distribution rather than a probability classifier:

residual = actual_carries − oof_carries
shortfall = book_line − oof_carries
P(over) = P(residual > shortfall)

Residuals are stratified by predicted carry bucket (lt5 / 5–9 / 10–14 / 15–19 / 20+) because the regression model has known bias at extremes. Within each bucket, a Gaussian KDE is fit to the residuals and the CDF is evaluated at the shortfall. This correctly accounts for fat tails rather than assuming a normal distribution.

Key Findings

  • QB under (line ≥ 6.5) IS ROI = 9.98% — strongly positive in-sample. The strategy is not a lucky OOS streak — it exists in the full training data too.
  • IS/OOS ratio = 0.71× — OOS actually outperforms IS. This is a strong anti-overfit signal. The model's in-sample predictions are more accurate (less uncertainty), so fewer rows generate extreme shortfalls and the edge filter selects slightly less confidently. The structural market mispricing (QB rush props overpriced) is the dominant driver, not model fit.
  • IS win rate (58.9%) vs OOS win rate (61.2%): The OOF model selects higher-quality under bets because its predictions have more noise — paradoxically, the less accurate OOF predictions create larger shortfalls which generate higher-confidence P(under) signals. The full-data model is more conservative about declaring "this is definitely an under."
  • Low-line QB under at edge≥0.15 shows IS ROI of 47% (74 bets) — flagged below as likely small-sample noise.
