## The 2025 offense: historically bad

The Browns ranked #448 of 448 teams in yards before contact per RB run over 14 seasons of data (0.38 yards — 3.3x worse than NFL average). Their offense ranked dead last in literally every meaningful metric:

- #32 EPA/pass, pass success rate, YPA, first downs per pass, passer rating
- #32 EPA/rush, rush success rate, first downs per rush
- #32 red zone drive rate, points per drive (1.29), touchdowns per drive (13%)
- #31 average halftime lead; #32 yards per drive (22.0)

Their leading receiver was a 21-year-old rookie tight end (Harold Fannin Jr.). The wide receiver room produced the fewest receiving yards in the NFL.

---

## The 2025 defense: elite

The defense was the opposite — arguably the best in football on a per-drive basis:

- **#2 in total defensive efficiency**; **#2 vs. pass**; **#2 vs. run**
- **#1 defense in football on a per-drive basis**
- 53 sacks recorded (#3); #8 pressure rate
- Myles Garrett: 23 sacks (broke NFL single-season sack record Week 18); 33 TFL (2nd-most in NFL history); AP Defensive Player of the Year (unanimous, 50/50 first-place votes)
- Carson Schwesinger: AP Defensive Rookie of the Year (as a 33rd overall pick)
- Defense gave up only 24 points per game despite the worst possible field position from the offense

This elite defense couldn't help the Browns win more than 5 games because the offense was so catastrophically bad. The "defense wins championships" thesis was fully stress-tested in Cleveland and failed.

## The Myles Garrett trade

The Browns traded Garrett to the Rams at his peak value. Return:
- Jared Verse (25-yr old EDGE; 2024 DROTY; 20.7% pressure rate; led Rams in pressure-over-expectation)
- 2027 1st-round pick
- 2028 2nd-round pick
- 2029 3rd-round pick

Verse is a legitimate pass rusher. The Browns lose the irreplaceable double-team gravity of the most dominant EDGE in football, but accumulate draft capital to rebuild.

## The QB catastrophe

The Browns have used these starting QBs since 2022:
Deshaun Watson (2022), Jacoby Brissett (2022), Watson (2023), Jeff Driskel (2023), Joe Flacco (2023), PJ Walker (2023), Dorian Thompson-Robinson (2023), Jameis Winston (2024), Watson (2024), Bailey Zappe (2024), Thompson-Robinson (2024), Flacco (2025), Dillon Gabriel (2025), Shedeur Sanders (2025).

Watson is projected starter per betting markets but:
- Missed all of 2025 re-rupturing his Achilles **during rehab**
- Has played only **19 total games since 2020**
- In 7 seasons since 2019, never won more than 5 games in any season
- Among 59 QBs over 5 years: #59 EPA/play (-0.16), #59 sacks/pressure (26%), #56 success rate (37%), #56 INT+sack% (12%), #55 YPA (6.0), #52 comp% (61%)

Sanders finished 2025 ranked: #45 completion rate, #43 EPA/dropback, #43 success rate, #42 sack+INT rate, #32 YPA — among 45 qualifying QBs. His 0.7 TD:INT ratio ranked 44th.

## The OL rebuild

After allowing the highest pressured dropback rate in the NFL, Cleveland is starting over with 6 new starters. They invested significantly: Spencer Fano (#9 overall), Zion Johnson ($49.5M), Elgton Jenkins, Tytus Howard (trade). The issue: Fano is an unknown who may shift to unfamiliar positions, and rebuilding an entire OL from scratch in one offseason rarely produces immediate results.

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- Defense was #1 in football per drive — even without Garrett, Verse + Schwesinger + Mason Graham form a legitimate core
- Schedule is 4th easiest; teams earn easy schedules by being bad
- Fumble luck was -3.3 net last year (#27) — positive regression expected
- OL got completely rebuilt with premium capital; can only go up from the worst OL season in 14 years of data
- Todd Monken ran Lamar Jackson's historic offense — he understands what good football looks like

**Under argument**
- QB room is existential uncertainty: Watson hasn't been a functional NFL QB in years; Sanders was historically bad as a rookie; Gabriel is a floor-raising backup at best
- OL rebuilding with 6 new starters doesn't fix itself in Year 1 — new units take time
- Losing Garrett's double-team gravity impacts every other defensive player, not just the sack numbers
- Receivers still #30; Jeudy regressed; the rookies (Concepcion, Boston) are untested
- 6.5 wins means significant offensive improvement from literally the worst offense in modern NFL history

**Sharp lean**: Under. The defense keeps a floor, but the offense needs to go from historically worst to league average. With a QB room of Watson (broken), Sanders (rookie flameout), and Gabriel (ceiling-less), that improvement is unlikely. 6.5 wins requires substantial positive steps from multiple units simultaneously.

## Prop angles

- **Harold Fannin Jr.**: Target share over in first half of season — he is the most reliable pass-catcher on the roster and Monken will need a safety valve immediately. Under on full-season volume if Concepcion/Boston claim roles by midseason.
- **Shedeur Sanders**: Fade all passing props — accuracy concerns, weak OL, worst supporting cast in the NFL. If he starts, expect sack rate spikes and turnover-prone play.
- **Jared Verse**: Sacks and pressure props over — he's a legitimate pass rusher who benefited from double coverage at LA but won't get the same gravity. Realistic 12-15 sack range.
- **Quinshon Judkins**: Volume props are OL-dependent — the OL is still #32; until the line proves functional, fade rush attempts in early weeks.
- **Game totals**: Low totals in Browns games — offense-dependent total leans Under unless facing a weak defense.
