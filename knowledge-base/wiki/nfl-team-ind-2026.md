## The injury collapse: 8-2 → 8-9

**Week 10 at Falcons (Germany)**: Jones takes 7 sacks, 8 hits. Game ends in OT, Colts win 31-25.

Week 12 (vs Chiefs): Jones plays "Full" per injury report but clearly compromised — 4.5 YPA, 61% comp, Colts lose 23-20.

**Week 14 at Jacksonville**: Jones tears Achilles in right leg (the compensation leg for the fibula fracture he had been hiding). Season over.

Colts finished **0-5 from Week 14-18**, including 44-year-old Philip Rivers un-retiring for one start.

**Split with vs. without Jones (fully healthy)**:
- Weeks 1-10 (healthy): 8-2
- Weeks 12-18 (injured/post-injury): 0-7

Against bottom-16 teams by efficiency: 7-0. Against top-16 teams: 1-9. The schedule context matters: the 8-2 start was largely against weak opponents.

## The Sauce Gardner trade catastrophe

Indianapolis sent the Jets:
- **2026 1st-round pick**
- **2027 1st-round pick**
- Adonai Mitchell (WR)

Return: Sauce Gardner, who debuted the same game Jones fractured his fibula, then suffered a calf injury and played only 4 games.

The Colts also have Charvarius Ward (concussions limited him to 7 games). They have two of the best cornerbacks in the NFL, neither of whom has been healthy enough to play together.

## The defense problem

Despite playing the **#6 easiest schedule of offenses**, Indianapolis ranked:
- **#28 early down success rate** allowed
- **#24 total defense**
- **#32 red zone drive rate allowed** (38% — the highest in the NFL)
- **#31 forced punt rate** (27% — near the bottom)
- **#27 opponent scoring drive rate** (44%)

Anarumo's bend-but-don't-break philosophy bent constantly. The underlying yardage numbers were ugly across the board.

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- Shane Steichen's offense is historically elite in Q1 and red zone (regardless of QB — his system creates structure)
- Jones cleared for 7-on-7 by early June 2026 — optimistic trajectory for Week 1 availability
- Alec Pierce is now a legitimate #1 WR on a $116M deal; Tyler Warren as TE1 is a weapon
- Jonathan Taylor is elite when a functional QB is on the field
- O-Line is #11 and returns intact; RBs ranked #4
- Secondary (Gardner + Ward) could be elite if both healthy simultaneously

**Under argument**
- Jones is a torn Achilles recovery — 6-8 months optimistic puts him at training camp or Week 1 at best; standard range suggests missing early weeks
- Even when Jones came back from 2024 injuries, he wasn't the same player; this is an Achilles on his compensation leg
- Defense was genuinely bad (#28 early down, #32 RZ drives allowed) on the 6th easiest schedule — gets harder in 2026
- No 1st-round draft pick (Sauce Gardner trade burned it) — can't upgrade roster with premium capital
- AFC South is improving; being underdogs in 11 games is the market's assessment
- Richardson and Leonard are the fallback QBs; without Jones, this is a 5-6 win team

**Sharp lean**: Neutral, slight Under. The offense with a healthy Jones is genuinely elite. But an Achilles recovery at his age and injury history, combined with a defense that was legitimately bad on an easy schedule, leaves too much risk on the downside. 7.5 wins requires Jones to be healthy from Week 1 and the defense to improve.

## Prop angles

- **Daniel Jones**: Do not bet TDs over or YPA over until health is confirmed Week 1. If he plays Week 1 and looks healthy, his EPA/att and YPA are legitimate top-10 numbers in Steichen's system.
- **Jonathan Taylor**: Rush attempts over in first half of season — Steichen will run the ball more when Jones is healthy (passing game credibility). Fade rush efficiency stats (YPC over) — Taylor's 6.0 YPC was a mirage fueled by light boxes; healthy Jones needed for that.
- **Tyler Warren**: TE target share over — he is the #1 TE on the roster and Steichen's offense featured him heavily as a rookie; his Pro Bowl ceiling is real.
- **Alec Pierce**: Route-adjusted metrics suggest he is a legitimate deep threat; YPA over is reasonable on a per-catch basis if he stays healthy.
- **Game totals**: Lean Under on Colts games until Jones' Week 1 status is clear. Steichen's offense scores plenty with a functional QB; without one, the Colts are below average.
