## The 13-4 season: earned but annotated

Jacksonville's 2025 was a legitimate improvement — and also heavily annotated by luck:

**Non-offensive TDs in key wins**:
- 99-yd INT return vs Chiefs (won by 3 — without it, likely a loss)
- 87-yd punt return vs 49ers (won by 5 — without it, likely a loss)
- 73-yd punt return vs Texans
- 58-yd INT return vs Titans

**Other luck factors**:
- **3-0 in FG margin games (100%, #1 NFL)** — no team maintains perfect record in field-goal-margin games
- **2-0 in overtime** (#1 best mark)
- **+3.5 fumble luck (#8)**; **+3.0 FG luck (#4)**
- **Faced backup QB parade**: Brandon Allen (TEN), Philip Rivers, Brady Cook (NYJ), Riley Leonard (IND), Cam Ward (TEN), Jacoby Brissett (ARI), Davis Mills (HOU) — 7 of their wins came against backup QBs or teams that finished below .500
- **Played #5 easiest schedule of offenses**; run defense played **#4 easiest schedule of rush offenses**

**The historic takeaway jump**:
- 2024: **10 takeaways total** (10th fewest in NFL history)
- 2025: **31 takeaways (#2 NFL)**, 22 interceptions (#2 NFL), 107 passes defensed (franchise record)
- **FIRST TEAM IN NFL HISTORY** to produce 30+ takeaways after 10 or fewer the year before

**One year later, defense went from scoring 25 points off turnovers → 110 points off turnovers (#1 NFL)**. This kind of swing is structurally unrepeatable.

## Trevor Lawrence's 2025 context

Through Week 11, Lawrence ranked:
- #33/38 in completion rate (59.8%)
- #31/38 in TD:INT ratio (11.8)
- #27/38 in YPA (6.4)
- #34/38 in passer rating (79.4)

Then the schedule opened up — 4 straight games vs bottom-10 pass defenses, then 6 of 7 to close the season vs bottom-10 pass defenses. Lawrence went nuclear:
- Multiple TDs in 6 of final 7 games
- 62% of his passing TDs came in final 41% of season
- Lawrence finished 17th in EPA/play, 16th in YPA (7.2), 12th in passing TD rate (5.2%)

If you remove drops (Lawrence ranked **#1 in receiver errors** before the bye at 12.4%): #15 EPA/pass, #12 success rate, #13 YPA — legitimately above-average when stripped of context.

**Sharp take**: Coen is clearly helping Lawrence. The later-season surge was real. But stat padding against bottom-10 defenses and backup QBs inflated the counting stats.

## The Travis Hunter bet

GM James Gladstone traded from #5 to #4 (costing a second, a third, and a **2026 1st-round pick**) to select Travis Hunter.

Hunter played 7 games before tearing his LCL on October 30. He graded out at **73.2 PFF on defense, 62.2 on offense**. Reports suggest **he will play primarily CB in 2026**, not WR.

The 2026 draft without the #5-equivalent pick was damaging: Gladstone had the **#4 worst draft capital over expectation** score in 2026, taking players 30-100+ spots earlier than consensus value.

## The RB collapse

Travis Etienne averaged 4.3 YPC on 260 rushes with a 35% success rate — well below average. But he handled **70% of backfield touches** and was the engine. After his injury late, the Jaguars had nothing.

For 2026, Bhayshul Tuten (3.7 YPC, 47% success in 83 carries) starts at RB1, backed by Chris Rodriguez Jr. (who produced for Coen at Kentucky). **RB unit is #29** — this is a real weakness.

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- Lawrence + Coen is a real foundation — the late-season version of Lawrence is genuinely above-average
- Defense was legitimately elite: 31 takeaways, #5 EPA per pass allowed, #5 points per drive — the core is intact (Hines-Allen, Walker, Hamilton, Brown, Hunter when healthy)
- Schedule ease for rush defense (from #4 easiest to moderate) keeps run defense numbers respectable
- Travis Hunter Year 2 as a full-time player could be transformative at CB

**Under argument**
- **ESPN Pain Index #32** — worst schedule/travel combination since 2002; 11 road or neutral games, back-to-back road swings, two London trips, 7 of final 10 road
- 13-4 built on 4 non-offensive TDs in key wins, 3-0 in FG margin games (unrepeatable), 1 in NFL-historic takeaway jump, backup QB parade, and #5 easiest offense schedule
- Etienne (70% of touches) gone; RB unit is #29
- Penalties: **#29 in per-game average (128 total)** — top-3 penalized offense; historically penalized teams in 2025 mostly missed the playoffs
- Sack margin was **-9** (allowed more sacks than recorded) — pass protection has issues
- 4th quarter point margin: **-14 (#20)** — Jaguars blow leads
- OL #17 with a rookie trying to start at LG

**Sharp lean**: Under. The 13-4 was real but annotated. Regression in turnovers, FG luck, return TDs, and schedule difficulty alone should cost 3-4 wins. The rest/travel structure is legitimately the worst in the league. Lawrence and Coen are building something — but 9.5 wins requires repeating historic luck.

## Prop angles

- **Trevor Lawrence**: TD over on full-season basis — scheme and development are real; but early-season props are risky given the tough early schedule (at DEN Wk2 is altitude adjustment)
- **Brian Thomas Jr.**: Target share and yardage over — he is the legitimate #1 WR when healthy; the Meyers midseason trade targeted slot, freeing Thomas as primary X. If healthy, 85+ catches are his floor.
- **Jakobi Meyers**: Reception over in second half of season — his role as team's primary target from Week 10 on (42 catches, 483 yds, 61 targets) should carry into 2026.
- **Travis Hunter**: Interceptions over for full season — if he plays 13+ games at CB, his ball-hawk traits (7 INTs in 2 college seasons) translate.
- **Josh Hines-Allen**: Sacks over — #5 in pressure rate in 2025; legitimate double-digit sack candidate.
- **Game totals**: Lean Under on Jacksonville road games — they have 11 road/neutral games and the traveling starts in Week 2 at Denver. Team scored 1.4 pts/drive in Q1 on the season (14th). Road games after London trips or short weeks should be faded.
