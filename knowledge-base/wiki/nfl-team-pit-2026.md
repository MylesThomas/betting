## Aaron Rodgers: the diminishing returns

Among the last three Steelers starting QBs (Rodgers 2025, Fields 2024, Wilson 2024), Rodgers ranked **last in virtually every metric**:

- Did NOT lead in: completion rate, accuracy, YPA, success rate, passer rating, explosive pass rate, first-down rate, 3rd/4th down, inside RZ, between numbers, outside numbers, short throws, intermediate throws, deep throws
- Rodgers was barely better in EPA/att (+0.01 vs. -0.01 Fields, -0.02 Wilson)
- 2025 Rodgers vs. 2025 league: #36 in Y/A (6.7), #37 in EPA/att, #33 in success rate, #31 in INT%, #29 in TD:INT ratio, #33 in 3rd down conversion

**The style explains the numbers**: Rodgers' self-preservation instinct drives every decision. His 2.52-second release (fastest in NFL) and 6.0 air yards target depth (shortest) ensure he avoids sacks (5.5% rate, #15) but eliminate the deep game. Only 25.5% of his throws went 10+ yards downfield — lowest in the NFL. He threw behind the line of scrimmage on 31.7% of attempts — highest in the NFL.

The Steelers' offense was not built for Rodgers. Rodgers made the Steelers build the offense for him.

## The turnover voodoo

The only reason Pittsburgh was 10-7:

- **Turnover margin: +12 (#4)**
- **Fumble net FROE: +5.1 (#2)** — recovered 12 opponent fumbles (#2 in NFL)
- **Turnover net EPA: +83.05 (#2)**
- **1-score record: 7-3 (.700, #4)**; 70% of wins came by 1 score (#6 — highest share in league)

Strip away turnovers, and this was a middling team. Add them back in, and they were a division champion.

Under Tomlin, Pittsburgh won 22 of 30 close games (possession or fewer) over 3 seasons. The voodoo ran deep — and Tomlin is now gone.

## Defense: expensive but not dominant

The #1 most expensive defense in NFL ($186.57M total cap, $175M on defense). The production was average:
- **3rd down defense: 42% converted (#25)** — worst since 2009; +0.11 EPA/play on 3rd down (#29)
- **4th quarter: #29 EPA, #30 success, #32 score rate/drive (60.4%), #32 drives reaching RZ**
- Total defense: #15 success rate, #19 EPA per play, #19 yards per play

The front 7 and secondary rank #7 each — but neither unit performed like a top-7 unit in 2025.

One bright spot: **48 sacks recorded (#6)** — the pass rush infrastructure (Watt/Highsmith/Herbig + Heyward) remains genuine.

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- OL quietly excels: ranked #28 last year but **#3 in pass-block rate (71%)** and #9 in run-block rate (72%) — keeps Rodgers clean without premium cost
- Pittman + Metcalf give Rodgers two legitimate weapons; Bernard adds slot versatility
- Front 7 (#7) + Secondary (#7) retain the defensive infrastructure
- Rodgers' ball-control style avoids turnovers (7 INTs, 5.5% sack rate); the offense will sustain drives even if it doesn't push deep
- Schedule for opposing offenses is bottom-10; defense gets friendly matchups
- Negated bye #1 best — no scheduling disadvantage there

**Under argument**
- Rodgers at 42, ranked last among Steelers QBs in 3 years across nearly every metric — the decline is structural, not circumstantial
- Facing **#3 toughest schedule of pass defenses** (vs #17 in 2025) — the offense must dramatically improve against better competition
- Turnover luck (+12, #2 fumble recovery) is highly regressive year-to-year; losing that edge collapses the record
- 1-score record (.700) is statistically unsustainable; losing Tomlin removes whatever execution edge existed
- Receivers room is **#22** — Metcalf coming off career-worst season, Pittman averaged 9.8 YPC (#127 among WRs)
- Defense is #1 most expensive but was average in 2025 across meaningful efficiency metrics; spending doesn't guarantee results
- "Kenneth Gainwell was voted team MVP" — the best player on this team was a minimum-salary running back who is now in Tampa

**Sharp lean (Grade A — fascinating but built for mediocrity)**: "The entire formula feels built to prevent collapse, not create upside... far more likely to extend the illusion than finally break the cycle, which will leave the Steelers not just a season away from a rebuild, but kick the can of a seventh Super Bowl Championship further down the road." Slight Under. The Mike Tomlin departure removes the organizational engine that manufactured wins in tight games. The Cover 2/Zone-heavy defense faces better offenses. And Rodgers is 42 and getting worse at every measurable.

## Prop angles

- **DK Metcalf**: Receiving yards — likely to have more competition for targets from Pittman, but Rodgers' quick-release style concentrates volume on primary reads; over on targets but not necessarily yards
- **Jaylen Warren**: Rush yards over — Warren had career highs; Dowdle's role is likely complementary early-down; Warren remains the lead back
- **TJ Watt**: Sacks over — McCarthy's system gives the defense flexibility to use Watt more situationally; the pass-rush infrastructure still works
- **Aaron Rodgers**: Passing yards over — the volume is guaranteed with his quick-release style (498 attempts in 2025); 3,500+ yards is likely even at diminished quality
- **Game totals**: Lean Under in road games vs top-10 pass defenses; the offense cannot push the ball downfield against real competition
