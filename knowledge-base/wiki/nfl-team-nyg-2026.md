## Jaxson Dart: the signal underneath the catastrophe

The 4-13 record tells almost none of the useful story. When Wilson's first three starts are removed:
- **#8 in yards per drive** (was #28 in 2024)
- **#10 in rate of drives reaching red zone** (was #26)
- **#11 in touchdowns per drive** (was #30)
- **#13 in points per drive** (was #31)

That's one of the most dramatic offensive improvements in the NFL — in a season where the QB played:
- Without WR1 Malik Nabers (ACL, Week 4) for all but 3 games
- Without RB Cam Skattebo (ankle/fibula/ligament, Week 8) for the second half
- Without a legitimate head coach (Daboll fired Week 10)
- With a concussion evaluation 5 times in 10 games

**The specific Dart highlights**:
- **Rushing EPA**: 17.5 EPA on designed runs (#2 among 2025 rookies, behind only Josh Allen) — more than Lamar Jackson or Jayden Daniels in their rookie years
- **Combined rushing EPA**: 46.34 total (#3 among rookies since 2000, behind Daniels and Cam Newton)
- **3rd down**: +0.13 EPA/att (#6 in NFL) — tied with Justin Herbert as #1 rookie on 3rd down since 2000 in that class
- **Big-time throw rate**: 5.2% (#8 in entire NFL) — genuinely high-value throws
- **Passing EPA total**: -15.09 (#28 of 36 QBs) — the weakness that must improve

**The concussion problem**: 5 evaluations across 10 games. Dart's style (aggressive scrambling, takes reckless contact) is incompatible with a long career if unchanged. He publicly rejected changing his approach. Harbaugh's job is to preserve what makes Dart dynamic while removing the recklessness.

## The Dexter Lawrence trade

The Giants traded Lawrence to Cincinnati for the #10 overall pick (Mauigoa). The cost:

Lawrence **off** the field in 2025 (limited snaps, "down year"):
- #32 Y/A, #31 pressure rate, #31 pass success, #30 EPA/pass, #30 sack rate

Lawrence **on** the field in 2025:
- #13 Y/A, #15 pressure rate, #8 pass success, #10 EPA/pass, #8 sack rate

He was still the best interior lineman on the team when healthy. The front seven drops from #4 → #17 without him.

## Malik Nabers: the 2026 binary

Nabers underwent a second right knee surgery in April 2026 to remove scar tissue. Reports entering summer indicate recovery "has not been clean." He could begin the 2026 regular season on the PUP list, sidelining him for at least the first four to six games.

If Nabers misses significant time, Dart is again asked to carry too much too soon — a team that looks like a Wild Card contender on paper reverts toward the 2025 floor.

If Nabers plays from Week 1, Dart operates with his #1 weapon for the first time since three games into his rookie year.

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- 1-7 in one-score games (.125) is the 31st-worst mark in NFL history; mathematical regression to ~.500 adds 3-4 wins on its own
- Schedule drops from #1 toughest to 12th-hardest — same roster would likely win 7-8 games with this schedule
- Harbaugh is a genuine elite HC; his arrival immediately raises culture and late-game execution
- OL improved significantly (Mauigoa, Thomas healthy, Eluemunor) — protecting Dart reduces concussion risk and improves passing numbers
- Cam Skattebo returns; when healthy he was #1 among Giants RBs in efficiency and transformed the ground game
- Kicking overhaul (Sanders + Stout) fixed the 87.8% XP rate and poor FGA operation directly

**Under argument**
- Secondary is **#28** — the worst unit on the team; Greg Newsome II is a low-risk, not high-reward, addition
- Nabers on the PUP list for first 4-6 weeks would mean Dart continues in a compromised environment
- Harbaugh is categorized as a "game manager" head coach; the historical turnarounds from 4-13 to contender have come from coordinators who are scheme specialists (Vrabel, Dan Quinn, Flores), not game managers
- Dexter Lawrence is gone — front seven dropped from #4 to #17; pass rush improvement from Bailey is speculative
- Dart's concussion history (5 evaluations in 10 games) is a structural health and performance risk
- Receivers are **#26** — even with Nabers healthy, depth is Slayton + veterans on decline curves

**Sharp lean (Grade C)**: "In reality, they are at least a year away from legitimate contender tier status. If everything aligns in their favor, the Giants can finish around .500 and possibly push for a Wild Card spot." Neutral — 7.5 wins is achievable. The path to Over requires Nabers healthy from Week 1 and Dart's concussion history to be manageable. The path to Under requires Nabers PUP and the defense to continue surrendering late leads.

## Prop angles

- **Malik Nabers**: PUP-adjusted props — if he's confirmed healthy Week 1, target over on receptions aggressively; Dart's 3rd-down efficiency and big-time throw rate suggest he can feed a genuine WR1; but any PUP period makes full-season props risky
- **Isaiah Likely**: TE over early — if Nabers is on PUP, Likely becomes the primary receiving option; he's a top-5 TE when targeted; Harbaugh's system historically maximizes the TE position (he ran Mark Andrews)
- **Jaxson Dart**: Rushing yards over — 86 rushes as a rookie is the template; Nagy won't fully suppress it; the rushing production is repeatable and scheme-agnostic
- **Cam Skattebo**: Health-contingent; if confirmed healthy, rush attempts over in second half of season; his efficiency metrics with this OL are elite
- **Game totals**: Lean Under early-season home games while secondary remains #28 and offense stabilizes; Lean Over later-season games if Nabers is healthy and NFC East defenses are tired
