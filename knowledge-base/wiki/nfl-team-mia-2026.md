## The teardown: unprecedented scale

The Dolphins aren't just rebuilding — they are executing one of the most complete roster purges in modern NFL history:

- Over **60% of offensive snaps** and **~55% of defensive snaps** are new players vs 2025
- Any turnover exceeding 35% on one side is considered a major rebuild; 50%+ on both sides is "nearly unprecedented"
- **$180 million in dead cap** (prior record: $127.7M) — 60% of salary budget goes to players not on the roster
- GM Chris Grier fired Halloween 2025 (team 2-7); HC Mike McDaniel fired January 2026 (4 seasons, 35-33)
- New GM: **Jon-Eric Sullivan** (from Green Bay); new HC: **Jeff Hafley** (from Green Bay)

**The 2026 Dolphins are not trying to win.** Ownership won't publicly acknowledge it, but the front office has one eye on the **quarterback-rich 2027 NFL Draft**.

## Tua Tagovailoa: revisionism required

Tua's 2025 collapse (-0.01 EPA) masks a historically elite 2022-2024 run. Among 64 qualifying QBs across those three seasons:
- **#2 in Y/A (8.1)**
- **#2 in completion rate (68%)**
- **#3 in EPA per attempt (+0.17)**
- **#3 in success rate (49%)**
- **ONLY QB in the NFL to rank top 5 in BOTH EPA and success rate in all three seasons**

The 2025 collapse (time to throw increased from 2.3s to 2.6s; 7 INTs on quick passes) likely had multiple causes: the concussion history, the OL forcing him to hold longer, and the inability to use the quick-release scheme that defined his peak. Sharp doesn't fully exonerate or condemn him — but the 2022-2024 stretch was genuinely elite and underappreciated.

## Malik Willis: the game-state trap

Willis's aggregate stats from Green Bay (89 attempts: +0.37 EPA, 10.9 Y/A, 79% comp) are misleading for one structural reason:

**LaFleur ran the ball 61-74% of the time with Willis**, meaning Willis only threw when the defense was off balance. Love threw 56% of the time overall; Willis 39%.

With Willis, the Packers passed only 26% of the time on early-down first-half plays. With Love: 51%.

The game-state dependency runs deeper:
- **Willis has NEVER won a game where opponents scored 15+ points** (0-6 in career)
- His wins: 16-10, 17-10, 30-14 — all low-scoring team wins
- Red zone with Willis: **-0.19 EPA/att, 29% success rate, -0.30 EPA/play on all plays**
- Red zone with Love: +0.20 EPA/att, 44% success rate, +0.12 EPA/play on all plays

The Miami defense will surrender 20+ points to Purdy, Mahomes, Murray, Burrow, and others routinely. The game state Willis depends on to win will almost never materialize against this schedule.

Additionally, Willis has an **11% sack rate** (Love: 3.9%) — and Miami's OL ranked #29.

## De'Von Achane: the one thing worth betting

Achane's 2025 was one of the best RB seasons in recent NFL history:
- **238 carries, 1,350 yards, 8 TDs, 5.7 YPC — PFF #1 (91.0) among 55 qualifying RBs**
- 49 missed tackles forced; 4.1 YAC per attempt
- 67 catches on 85 targets; 488 receiving yards, 4 TDs
- Did this **behind a #27 run-block OL**, **without Tyreek Hill most of the season**, and with Tagovailoa throwing league-leading INTs

Achane did this in the hardest possible environment. He is the only offensive weapon that survives the teardown.

## Betting angles

**Over argument**
- Malik Willis: +0.37 EPA/att, 10.9 Y/A, 79% comp in small sample at GB — if team protects him with heavy run rate, he can manufacture surprise wins
- De'Von Achane's dual-threat value (rushing + receiving) is generational; Slowik comes from a coaching tree that uses RBs maximally
- Kadyn Proctor (#12 overall) is a high-ceiling immediate starter; OL rebuild could surprise in Year 1
- Willis's dual-threat ability adds a dimension (14.5% scramble rate with GB) that defenses must account for
- Favored in **only 1 game all year** (average line +6.9); opens as heavy dogs in most games — live-dog value is real in individual games

**Under argument**
- **QB, OL, WR, Front 7, Secondary all bottom-5 in the league**
- **WR room is #32** — Tyreek Hill + Jaylen Waddle are gone; what's left is Malik Washington and unproven rookies
- Willis: **0-6 when opponents score 15+ points**; the schedule guarantees opponents will score 20+ nearly every week
- Schedule jumps from #5 easiest to **#2 toughest**; rest structure jumps from #2 best to #3 worst — double-barrel headwind
- **$180M dead cap** means no ability to respond to in-season needs; the roster can only improve slowly as dead money clears (benefit comes in 2027-2028)
- Front 7 and Secondary are both **#32** — legitimately the worst defense in the NFL entering a brutal QB schedule
- The team is openly positioning for the 2027 draft; wins cost draft capital; every incentive points toward a low win total

**Sharp lean**: Clear Under. 4.5 wins requires the Dolphins to win nearly every close game in a schedule with zero cupcakes after Week 1. The roster talent gap is real, the schedule is punishing, and the game-state Willis needs to win (defense holding opponents to 14 or fewer) won't happen with a #32 defense facing the AFC East/NFC gauntlet. This is a development year for the coaching staff and Proctor.

## Prop angles

- **De'Von Achane**: Rush attempts over and all-purpose yards over — he is the entire offense; Slowik's system will lean into him heavily; 300+ touch season is likely regardless of results
- **De'Von Achane**: Caution on TD over — red zone efficiency was poor in 2025 without a functional passing threat; defenses will stack 8-man boxes knowing Willis can't beat them downfield
- **Malik Willis**: Rushing yards over — his 14.5% scramble rate at GB is the highest in the NFL for a starter; in a run-heavy offense, Willis rushing adds to his total; but TD over is a fade (RZ -0.19 EPA with Willis at GB)
- **Game totals**: Lean Under on MIA home games — the offense won't score enough to push totals over; Lean Under on most road games except early blowout risk means Over is possible via garbage time
- **Live dog value**: Miami as 10+ point underdogs against top-tier offenses may have value — the Dolphins will generate some garbage-time offense and keep games closer than the spread suggests in pure total yards
