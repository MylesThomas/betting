Step 4 — ypred → P(over/under) Probability Conversion

2026-08-07 11:43:59 ET

Plain English

The Ridge model outputs a number — e.g. "Josh Allen will throw 275 yards." But the bet is binary: over or under the line (e.g. 266.5 yds)?

To turn a point prediction into a probability, we wrap it in a bell curve centered at 275 yds. Then P(over 266.5) = the area of that curve above 266.5. The wider the bell curve, the less certain the model is, and probabilities stay closer to 50/50.

Two parameters were tuned:
  • sigma — the width of the bell curve. Base = residual std of the model (72.65 yds — how wrong the model typically is). Grid searched 0.7×–1.3× to find the best-calibrated width.
  • Distribution shape — Normal (symmetric) vs Student-t (fatter tails, more room for outlier games like 94 yds or 359 yds in the same season).
Winner: t(df=3, sigma×1.3) — heavy-tailed Student-t, sigma inflated to 94 yds. The model was slightly overconfident at the base width, so widening it improved calibration.

Brier score 0.24953 vs naive baseline 0.25 (just guessing 50% every time) — barely better than random. Expected: the model has AUC ~0.53, so the underlying signal is modest. The conversion is correct; the edge comes from finding the small % of games where the model disagrees meaningfully with the market.

Method

  • OOF ypred from LOO-season CV (Ridge, C_core_mkt_start features)
  • Residual std (sigma_base): 72.65 yds
  • P(over line) = 1 − CDF((line − ypred) / sigma)
  • Grid search: Normal vs Student-t; sigma_mult ∈ [0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3]; df ∈ [3, 5, 8, 15, 30]
  • Evaluation metric: Brier score (lower = better calibration)
  • p_model clipped to [0.01, 0.99]

Step 4b — Correction / Revert (2026-08-11 04:37:17 ET)

The logistic method won on Brier score (0.25202 vs 0.26653) but is degenerate for betting purposes: all p_model predictions fell within 0.0089 std of 0.5 (p5–p95 = 0.4828–0.5118). A flat predictor near 0.5 trivially scores Brier ≈ 0.25 in a 50/50 market without having any useful signal.

Decision: revert to t(df=3, σ×1.3), which has std=0.1356 and p5–p95=0.270–0.732 — the spread needed to generate meaningful edges. Brier score is not a sufficient criterion here; a method must also produce variance in predictions. The distribution method has both.

Why This Pipeline Fails — Root Cause Analysis

1. Predictive signal is too weak.
Best single-feature AUC from Step 3a: ~0.536 (logistic) and ~0.591 (XGB) on attempts_roll_career. The multi-feature Ridge model achieved RMSE=72.53 vs. a baseline of 77.31 — a modest 6.5% improvement. Residual std after Ridge is ~72 yards. For a market with lines in the 220-yard range, this means 1-sigma error is ±33% of the line — too wide to bet confidently.

2. p_model variance is low (Step 4).
Even with t(df=3, sigma×1.3) inflation, p_model std=0.1356 and only spans 0.27–0.73 at the 5th/95th percentile. High-edge situations are sparse — few player-games clear the model's confidence threshold. The logistic method (Step 4b) was worse: std=0.0089, essentially predicting 0.49–0.51 for everything — confirming that ypred alone carries little signal at any given line value.

3. 2024 overfitting is a red flag, not a green light.
2024 was a strong season for UNDER bets on elite QBs. This may reflect a specific year's game environment (weather, defensive schemes, injury patterns) rather than a systematic edge. 2023 and 2025 bracket the outlier year with flat/negative results — the pattern doesn't generalize.

4. Passing yards is a hard market to beat.
Unlike sacks (rare events, fat tails) or tackles (snap-count driven), passing yards depend on game script: lead/trail situation, opponent pass rush, weather, play-calling, red zone opportunities. These factors are partially in the line already — the market has strong signal on game environment. Our feature set (rolling averages, starter flag, opponent rate) adds marginal predictive power beyond what the line itself encodes.
