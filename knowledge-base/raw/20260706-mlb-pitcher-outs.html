What was built

Wrote src/mlb_pitcher_outs_modeling/scripts/fetch_market_lines.py — idempotent backfill script that fetches pitcher_outs, h2h, and spreads per event in a single API call. Key design choices:

  • MARKETS per call: pitcher_outs,pitcher_outs_alternate,h2h,spreads — pitcher_outs_alternate confirmed zero historical coverage (verified Aug 2024 event). Dropped from config.
  • Per-event snapshot: commence_time − 1 hour (not a fixed 2pm ET — day games and West Coast games would get in-game or missing snapshots otherwise)
  • Consensus game odds (median decimal across all books) broadcast onto every player-prop row: consensus_home_moneyline, consensus_away_moneyline, home_run_line_point, away_run_line_point, home_run_line_odds, away_run_line_odds
  • Zero-row marker parquet written for events with no pitcher_outs data (permanent skip on re-runs)
  • Odds stored in decimal format (historical API default). Must convert to American in spine for team_moneyline_odds feature.
  • BUG FIXED (2026-07-06): game_date was derived from UTC timestamp causing West Coast games (start after midnight UTC) to land on the next calendar day. Fixed to use ET date. Historical S3 files have UTC-derived dates — spine builder will handle via date−1 fallback on join.

Key Findings

  1. Core market is very well calibrated (14.5–18.5): All calibration gaps are ≤ ±3pp at the main lines. The market is not systematically wrong at any core line value. Edge must come from the model, not from raw market bias.
  2. 18.5 shows small under bias (−3.2pp): At the highest well-sampled line, unders hit 62% vs 58.8% market expectation. Pitchers rarely complete 6+ innings (18.5 outs = 6.2 IP). This supports the UNDER hypothesis at high lines.
  3. 14.5 shows small over bias (+2.1pp): At the lowest main line, overs hit 59% vs 56.9% market expectation. When books post a low line, they may be underestimating the starter.
  4. Low lines (12.5: +20.2pp): Extreme over bias but n=109 — insufficient to conclude directionally. Could be selection effect (books only post 12.5 for injury returns or spot starters who end up pitching well).
  5. 17.5 is nearly perfect calibration (+0.1pp): This is the most common line (36% of rows) and the market has it right. No edge from blanket bias here.
  6. Vig is consistent (~8% across all lines) — no line-specific vig exploitation opportunity.

Pattern Summary

Miscalibration at meaningful sample sizes (n ≥ 1,000): None with |gap| > 5pp. The only notable gap is 18.5 under (+3.2pp) and 14.5 over (+2.1pp). The market is well-informed. This means the model's job is to identify specific pitchers at specific matchups where the probability differs from the market consensus — not to exploit a blanket structural mismatch.

Carry into feature engineering: Does the model add signal beyond market novig especially at 17.5 and 18.5 (the two highest-volume lines where the market may be anchoring on a single line)?

Step 4 — Probability Conversion (2026-07-07 08:12:11 ET)

2026-07-07 08:12:11 ET

Two methods compared. Both use 10,000 bootstrap samples drawn from historical OOF residuals per player-game (one shared sample set per player-game evaluated at all line values — guarantees monotonicity by construction).

Method A: yhat = consensus_line (no model)

Residuals = outs_recorded − consensus_line. P(under line) = fraction of bootstrap samples where consensus_line + residual < line. Residual mean ≈ −0.30–0.34 (pitchers slightly under-perform their posted lines on average).

Method B: yhat = LinearRegression(consensus_line)

Residuals = outs_recorded − yhat_model. OOF model trained on 2024 / 2024+2025, coef ≈ 0.83–0.86.
