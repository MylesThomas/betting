The market prices overs and unders nearly symmetrically — median implied P(over) = 50.0%, mean = 49.7%. But the actual over hit rate is only 45.2%. This means the market implies 50/50 on average, yet unders hit 54.8% of the time. The most likely explanation: lines are set at x.5 (half-point above round numbers), so when a player's true expected score is a whole number, the under (≤ x) hits more often than the over (≥ x+1) simply due to rounding. The market is not systematically mispriced — it is efficiently pricing the continuous distribution but rounding the line up slightly. This is an important prior entering Step 3: do not assume the 45/55 split is exploitable without a model that beats market-implied probability.

Step 1.5 — Market Calibration by Line

Timestamp: 2026-07-02 21:04:26 ET

Methodology

Joined props to actual game log PTS on (player_key, game_date). Deduplicated to one row per player-game-line (multiple books for same line collapsed). Grouped by line value and computed: actual over rate, actual under rate, market no-vig implied P(over), and calibration gap = actual_over_rate − market_implied_P(over). A gap > +5pp means market underprices overs (OVER value); gap < −5pp means market overprices overs (UNDER value).

Key Finding: Structural UNDER Value at Lines ≥ 21.5

At lines ≤ 20.5, calibration gaps are small (±5pp or less — within noise). At lines ≥ 21.5, the gap becomes consistently and increasingly negative: the market prices overs higher than they actually hit. This is the same "star player UNDER" effect identified in the NBA assists research. The gap grows monotonically as line increases, reaching −27.3pp at line 40.5.

  • Line 21.5: actual 42.6%, market implied 48.4% → gap −5.7pp
  • Line 26.5: actual 37.1%, market implied 46.1% → gap −9.0pp
  • Line 30.5: actual 31.3%, market implied 41.9% → gap −10.6pp
  • Line 35.5: actual 16.5%, market implied 36.6% → gap −20.1pp
  • Line 40.5: actual 6.3%, market implied 33.6% → gap −27.3pp

⚠ Odds Approximation Caveat

All PnL calculations use no-vig approximated odds (derived from novig_prob_over). Actual book odds include the vig (~7–8% at mainstream lines, ~8–9% at high lines). This means reported ROI figures are optimistic — actual production ROI will be lower. Rough impact:

  • No-vig UNDER odds: e.g. −108. Actual book UNDER odds: e.g. −115 to −125.
  • At "all lines + under + dog_only" (53.6% win rate): breakeven at −118 = 54.1%. Marginal — depends heavily on which book and exact odds. Likely 1–3% actual ROI.
  • At "high_25plus + under + dog_only" (54.9% win rate): breakeven at −120 ≈ 54.5%. This strategy is more robust to vig at high lines even though vig is higher (~8%), because the calibration gap (−10pp at line 35.5) is larger. Likely 2–5% actual ROI.

⚠ Steps 3–6: Approach Error — Redoing with Regression

What was wrong

Steps 3–6 above used a binary classification model predicting is_over (0/1) as the target. This is the wrong modeling approach for a variable-line market like NBA player points.

The fundamental problem: is_over = 1 if pts_actual > offered_line. Including offered_line as a feature in a classifier predicting is_over creates a near-tautology — the model learns that when offered_line is high, is_over tends to be 0. The resulting p_model (P(over) from the classifier) closely tracks p_market (novig_prob_over), leaving essentially no independent signal. AUC of 0.52 was the warning sign: barely above random.

Correct approach (matching NBA rebounds, NFL tackles, NFL rec yards pipelines)

  1. Regression model: predict pts_actual (continuous), yielding yhat per player-game via OOF walk-forward. Evaluation metrics: RMSE, MAE, R².
  2. Bootstrap P(under/over): for each player-game, draw N=10,000 samples from yhat + resample(training_residuals). P(under) = fraction of draws ≤ offered_line. This converts the regression point estimate into a probability using the empirical noise distribution. No classification threshold needed.
  3. Edge: edge_under = p_model_under − p_market_under; edge_over = p_model_over − p_market_over. Grid search over min_edge, direction, odds bucket, line bucket only — no clf_threshold dimension.

This is the same pattern as: src/nfl_rec_yards_modeling/scripts/infer.py (bootstrap from OLS residuals) and src/nba_rebounds_modeling/option_a_scoring.py (Normal CDF). We use bootstrap here since NBA points have some skew and the empirical residual distribution is more robust.

mean_adj = offered_line + (1 − shrinkage) × (yhat − offered_line)

Shrinkage physically moves mean_adj toward the offered line. S2 trusts the raw model (yhat = 27.71 stays as 27.71). S1 compromises halfway to the line (28.10).
