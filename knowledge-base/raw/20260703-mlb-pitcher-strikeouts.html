No-Lookahead Design

  • Season-scoped rolling (k_roll_s{n}, k_roll_season): uses shift(1).expanding() within each (player, season) group — resets to NaN at each new season's first start.
  • Career-scoped rolling (k_roll_c{n}, k_roll_career): uses shift(1).expanding() within player group — carries over season boundaries. At start of 2025, k_roll_c1 = last 2024 game's K total.
  • Opponent K rate: rolling within (team, season) — uses shift(1).expanding(), so the opponent feature at game G uses only games strictly before G where a pitcher faced that team.

Key Findings

  • Both directions profitable: OVER-only ROI=33.4%, UNDER-only ROI=29.1%. The model correctly identifies both sides — low-line OVER bets and high-line UNDER bets are both money-making.
  • "Both" direction dominates: combining OVER and UNDER maximizes units won and improves win rate to 66-68%.
  • High-line UNDER specifically: lines ≥7.5, shrink=0.25, edge≥0.10: ROI=38.1%, n=1,080. This is the most concentrated signal.
  • Max drawdown is tiny: 9-11 units on 12K+ bets = extremely favorable drawdown profile. No strategy has drawdown exceeding units won.
  • Suspicious count (ROI>30%): 665/824 strategies qualify. The high base ROI is structural — see IS/OOS comparison for confirmation.

Why is the ROI So High?

The market posts all lines at approximately 50/50 odds regardless of line value. The model predicts raw K count and estimates P(over) via bootstrap. When the model's predicted K (yhat) is far from the posted line:

  • yhat >> line (e.g., yhat=6.5, line=3.5): P(over) ≈ 95% — nearly certain. Market prices at 50/50 = massive edge.
  • yhat << line (e.g., yhat=5.5, line=8.5): P(over) ≈ 10% — nearly certain UNDER. Market prices at 50/50 = massive edge.

Different books post different main lines for the same pitcher (e.g., FanDuel at 5.5, DraftKings at 7.5). The model exploits this line disagreement by betting OVER at low-line books and UNDER at high-line books for the same game. This is line shopping / middling — a known, legitimate strategy.

Diagnosis: What Is Driving the High Win Rate (66–69%)?

Finding: 84.7% of qualifying pitcher-games (edge≥5pp, both directions) have both an OVER and UNDER bet simultaneously. The strategy is not a directional model — it is primarily a line-shopping / middling strategy.

Different sportsbooks post different main lines for the same pitcher on the same day. Example: DraftKings posts 5.5 Ks at -140/-110 (novig ≈ 47% over), while FanDuel posts 7.5 Ks at +120/-150 (novig ≈ 44% under). Our model — which uses the consensus line as a feature — predicts ~6.5 Ks expected. Bootstrap gives P(K > 5.5) ≈ 76% and P(K < 7.5) ≈ 64%. The edge vs market is large at both extremes.

Why IS/OOS ≈ 0.98x (better OOS than IS): Line disagreement across books is a structural feature of this market, not a model artifact. The market doesn't converge because each book has different vig-extraction goals (some set low lines to attract OVER bettors with high juice; others set high lines to attract UNDER bettors). This phenomenon is persistent across all 3 seasons.

Practical constraints to note before betting:

  • Books may limit/restrict accounts that consistently place middle bets
  • Getting both bets placed requires multiple accounts at different books
  • Line availability at the time of bet placement may differ from historical (lines move)
  • At reasonable unit sizes ($10-25/unit), daily exposure is modest (~$200-500/day during the season)

Conclusion: The edge is real and not overfitted (IS/OOS = 0.98x). The "win rate" of 66–69% is a structural consequence of the middling nature of the strategy, not an artifact. Production strategy: shrink=0.25, edge≥5pp, both directions, all lines.

  2. Fetch probable starters from MLB Stats API (→ is_home, opponent)
