Key findings

  • No pushes at any line — confirmed. Total bases is always an integer; all lines are at .5 values. over_rate + under_rate = 1.0000 exactly at every line.
  • Vig is consistent — ~7.6–7.9% across all actionable lines. No book/line-specific vig outliers.
  • 0.5 line: near-perfect calibration — gap of −0.0050. Market knows who will get any hit (56%); model at this line won't add much.
  • 1.5 line: mild over-mispricing — gap of −0.0234. Market prices 41.1% over; actual is 38.8%. Slight UNDER value at 1.5 for average batters. Not large enough to trade on alone.
  • 2.5–3.5 lines: growing over-bias — gaps of −0.04 to −0.05. Market consistently overprices OVERS at these lines. These are set for above-average hitters and the public loves betting them over.
  • 4.5 line: slight OVER underpricing (+0.014) — the 4.5 line is reserved for elite hitters (Soto, Freeman, Alvarez) who genuinely go over more than the market implies. Interesting reversal of the general trend.
  • 5.5 line: FLAGGED — strong UNDER value (gap = −0.0833) — market prices 38.5% over, actual is 30.1%. 8.3pp miscalibration. Best structural signal found. Likely driven by extreme recency bias on outlier big games for star players.
  • 6.5 line: FLAGGED — but n=67 only — gap is −0.178 but too few samples to draw conclusions. Worth watching.

Strategic hypothesis from Step 1.5

Primary: UNDER at 5.5+ lines. Market overprices overs for star sluggers at elevated lines, likely due to public betting bias. Gap of 8.3pp at 5.5 is larger than seen at any lower line.

Secondary: UNDER at 2.5–3.5 lines for above-average hitters. Gap 4-4.5pp, systematic across the range, consistent with the well-known "public OVER bias" hypothesis from Step 0.

Counter-hypothesis: Line selection is endogenous — the 5.5 line is only posted for players on exceptional recent form (e.g., a 3-HR game yesterday). Regression to the mean would explain the gap without any market inefficiency. The model must control for this via rolling averages before declaring the 5.5 edge real.

  • Higher edge thresholds (8pp, 10pp) produce n_is < 100 — too thin to evaluate
  • min_books=1 chosen over min_books=2: IS ROI nearly identical (+1.89% vs +1.87%), n_is +70% larger → more bets, lower variance
  • Line 2.5/3.5 UNDER: IS negative despite OOS sometimes positive — IS/OOS pattern consistent with noise at those lines, not structural edge

Why UNDER only? Why line 1.5?

  • Market systematically overprices OVERS (calibration gap at line 1.5: actual over rate 35.1% vs market novig 37.9% → -2.8pp). Bettors like rooting for hits; books shade accordingly.
  • Line 1.5 is the dominant market: 69,112 of 143,219 qualifying rows (48%). Line 0.5 UNDER is structurally wrong (actual under rate = 41.6% vs line 0.5 → most batters get ≥1 TB). Lines ≥ 2.5 have sparse market volume and inconsistent IS signal.
  • OVER strategies disqualified: all had either negative IS ROI OR were driven by the 2026-04-20 bad market data cluster. After filter, no OVER combo passed both IS>0 and OOS>0 at n≥100/30.

Regression Step 4 — P(over) Conversion

Why Not Normal CDF

Normal CDF assumption fails for total_bases — it's a right-skewed discrete count (42% = 0 TB, spike at 4 TB for home runs). With y_hat≈1.44 and σ=1.74, Normal gives P(under 1.5) ≈ 51% vs empirical 61%. The model would systematically underestimate P(under).

Calibration Approach

Per-line logistic regression: P(over line | y_hat) = sigmoid(a + b · y_hat). Trained on OOF data. Results:
