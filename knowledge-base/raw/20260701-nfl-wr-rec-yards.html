Market Type Analysis

Receiving yards is a variable/numeric line market. Unlike sacks (99%+ of lines at 0.5) or interceptions (binary), rec yards lines vary continuously — typically from ~20.5 for slot receivers and TEs in limited roles up to ~99.5 for elite WR1s in favorable matchups. This means:

  • Regression-based models (OLS + NegBin NB2) are appropriate, same as tackles
  • Edge is computed as P(model under) − P(market under) at a specific offered line
  • Different books offering different lines for the same player-game are genuinely different bets
  • There is likely value in UNDER bets given human bias toward rooting for success (OVERs on popular players)

Betting Hypothesis

Same contrarian thesis as sacks/tackles: books likely shade lines slightly high on popular WR1s (fans want to bet over Tyreek Hill, CeeDee Lamb, etc.), creating value on unders. Model should be able to identify players where snap%, target share, and game context point to lower yardage than the line implies.

Step 4 — Outcome Distribution → Binary Bet Signal

Hybrid Model Architecture

  • Lines < 20.5: Bootstrap resampling from OLS residuals (10,000 draws) → P(under)
  • Lines ≥ 20.5: NegBin NB2 CDF at floor(line) → P(under)
  • Edge: p_model − p_market (consensus devigged under prob across all books)

Surprise finding: The dominant strategy is OVER bets, not UNDER bets. When the model (which already has a +5pp UNDER bias) still thinks P(under) is less than the market price, these are genuine OVER opportunities — the market is pricing the under side so aggressively that even our under-biased model says "fade the under." Mean line for these OVER bets = 14.4–23.6 yards (low-line players where books shade aggressively toward under).

Why OVER Strategies Win

The key insight: for low-line players (14-24 yard lines, mostly slot WRs, TEs in run-heavy offenses, and "name" players on bad snap days), books tend to shade lines low to attract OVER action from casual fans betting on their favorite players. Our model correctly identifies when even its own under-biased prediction is HIGHER than the market's under probability — meaning the market has gone too far toward the over side. Wait, that's backwards — let me clarify:

  • Market prices: P(under) = 0.60 (aggressively priced under, e.g., line at 19.5 for a marginal player)
  • Model says: P(under) = 0.55 (model thinks under is less likely than the 60% the market claims)
  • Edge = 0.55 - 0.60 = -0.05 → bet OVER
  • Actual: player goes for 22 yards → OVER wins ✓

The market is overpricing the under at low lines (possibly because they know casual fans will bet the over on recognizable names). Our model catches this.
