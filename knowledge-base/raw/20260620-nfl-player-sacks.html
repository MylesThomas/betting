Step 1 — Data Pull + EDA

Completed: 2026-06-20 | Data: 2025 regular season

What Was Built

  • Pulled player_sacks market from s3://the-odds-api-mt/nfl/props_backfill/2025/ and all_markets/2025/
  • Loaded NFL defensive player-game stats via nfl_data_py + Pro Football Reference snap counts
  • Deduped to one Over row per player-game-book (latest snapshot); Under rows joined separately
  • 4 Week 18 games had zero market coverage: GB_MIN, DAL_NYG, NYJ_BUF, KC_LV — confirmed starters resting or irrelevant matchups

Key Calibration Finding

Market systematically overprices the Over. At 50–60% implied Over probability (i.e. the book thinks sacks is a near-coinflip), the actual Under hit rate is ~73%. Books are putting too much probability on a player getting a sack in a given game. This is the primary edge signal.

Book Coverage Note

FanDuel posts Over-only. FanDuel does not post Under lines for sacks — only the Over side is listed. This means FanDuel rows can only ever be OVER bets in the pipeline. BetOnline posts both sides and is the source of most Under bets.

Step 2 — Feature Engineering / Spine

Completed: 2026-06-20 through 2026-06-25 | Spine v3

What Was Built

  • Spine keyed on pfr_player_id (not player name) to prevent rolling stat mixing for same-named players
  • Rolling sack rate: L1, L3, L5, L8, L16, career (all prior games) — strict temporal shift, no leakage
  • QB hit rate (rolling L16): proxy for pass-rush productivity even when sacks don't convert
  • Snap count percentage (L8): time-on-field proxy from nfl_data_py snap data
  • Market implied probability: per-book Over implied (with vig) and median novig implied across books
  • Name normalization: strip suffixes (Jr./Sr./II–V), dot removal, lowercase; 11 alias mappings
  • Actuals from PFR box score (def_sacks) via pfr_player_id — eliminates name-matching bugs that silently zero out compound/suffix names

Step 3 — Model Training

Completed: 2026-06-25 | Model: M7 Parsimonious LR

Model Selection

Market type is skewed binary (99.4% at 0.5) — binary logistic regression is appropriate. XGBoost tested but showed no lift over LR at this sample size and signal strength. Parsimonious feature set chosen because the market already prices position and categorical factors.

Final Model — M7 Parsimonious

ParameterValue
Model classLogistic Regression (sklearn, normalized features)
Artifactlr_norm_2024_2025_v4.pkl
Training data2024+2025 combined (IS); 2024-only for OOS holdout
Targetis_over — 1 if sacks ≥ 1, 0 if sacks = 0 (pushes excluded)
Numeric featuresprop_median_impl_over, qbhit_rate_L16, sack_rate_Lcareer
Categorical featuresNone — market already prices position and context
Feature grainplayer-game (deduped before training; label invariant across books at 0.5 line)

Feature Rationale

FeatureRationale
prop_median_impl_overMarket consensus novig Over probability — the strongest single predictor; summarizes all public information about player context, matchup, and status
qbhit_rate_L16QB hits in recent 16 games — captures pass-rush productivity when sacks don't fully convert; less noisy than sack rate over short windows
sack_rate_LcareerCareer sack rate — stabilized long-run talent signal; insensitive to recent variance
Why no categorical features? Position group (outside/inside/other) and team context are already reflected in the market implied probability. Adding them as explicit features on top of prop_median_impl_over added noise without AUC gain in OOF evaluation. Parsimonious wins.

Accuracy Source for Actuals

Actuals use PFR box score (def_sacks) via pfr_player_id. Prior versions used PBP name-matching which silently zeroed out sacks for compound names (e.g. "Josh Hines-Allen") and suffix names (e.g. "Shaquil Barrett Jr."). Switching to PFR box score + pfr_player_id eliminated all silent zeroing bugs.

Step 4 — Scoring + Inference

Completed: 2026-06-26

What Was Built

  • Scored both 2024 and 2025 seasons; output stored as parquets for Steps 5–6
  • Pipeline grain: player-game-book (one row per player × game × bookmaker)
  • Per-book edges: edge_over = p_over − over_implied; edge_under = (1−p_over) − under_implied
  • p_over is identical across books for the same player-game (it's a model output, not per-book)
  • Edge is per-book because over_implied / under_implied vary by book

P&L Convention

OutcomeUnits
Win1 / implied − 1 (actual book decimal payout, not flat −110)
Loss−1

over_implied and under_implied in the scored parquet are raw book-implied probabilities (with vig, sum ~1.06). Payout = 1/implied − 1 gives the correct per-unit return at the actual posted price.
